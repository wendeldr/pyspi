## Documented definition (shared by all units)

Supplement §1.6.4, p.19 (heading at supplement.txt line 915). The keywords are "undirected, linear, unsigned, bivariate, time-dependent".

> "The envelope correlation [82, 83] is the correlation between the two amplitude envelopes of x and y. Power envelope correlation is computed using MNE, where we use six combinations of parameters, including whether the method was orthogonalized (modifier orth), the envelopes were squared and logs were taken prior to correlation (log), or the absolute of the correlation coefficient was used (abs)."

The references are [82] Hipp et al. 2012 (Nat Neurosci 15:884), [83] Khan et al. 2018 (NeuroImage 174:57) and [63] MNE v0.23.0. The GitBook page `spi-descriptions/miscellaneous.md:92-113` has the same text and lists all six identifiers.

The documentation leaves four things unstated:
- any frequency band or carrier frequency;
- the orthogonalisation formula;
- whether the non-log envelope is amplitude or power;
- whether "abs" is applied before or after the two orthogonalisation directions are averaged.

## Canonical definitions

- **Amplitude envelope correlation (AEC).** Bruns, Eckhorn, Jokeit & Ebner 2000, NeuroReport 11(7):1509-1514. It is the Pearson correlation over time of the Hilbert amplitude envelopes |x_f(t)| and |y_f(t)| of *band-limited* signals.
- **Orthogonalised power envelope correlation.** Hipp et al. 2012, Nat Neurosci 15(6):884-890, Methods. The orthogonalised signal is Y⊥X(t,f) = Im(Y(t,f) X*(t,f)/|X(t,f)|). It removes any component of Y that has zero phase lag relative to X, which suppresses volume-conduction or leakage effects. The paper correlates log-transformed power envelopes of X and Y⊥X, computes this in both orthogonalisation directions and averages them, separately for each carrier frequency (Morlet wavelets).
  - I give the "log-transformed" and "average both directions" details from memory. They match MNE's docstring and code, but I have not re-read them in the paper (see Open questions).
- **Absolute option.** The `absolute` option belongs to MNE (library default `absolute=True`, docstring `envelope.py:44-47`) and is associated with Khan et al. 2018. It is not part of Hipp 2012.

## What is implemented (common trace)

1. **Preprocessing.** `pyspi/data.py:184-203` optionally detrends, then z-scores (ddof=1). Configs are at `pyspi/config.yaml:1360-1391`. YAML labels are not read (`calculator.py:252-259`), so every instance has labels `["unsigned","misc","undirected"]` (probe).
2. **Wrapper.** `misc.py:168-176` moves the data from (P,T,1) to (1,P,T) and calls `envelope_correlation(z, orthogonalize=False|"pairwise", log, absolute)`. It then calls `.combine("mean")` (a single epoch, so identity; `mne/utils/check.py:1011-1016`) and `.get_data(output="dense")`, then squeezes and sets a NaN diagonal. This override bypasses `Undirected.multivariate` (REVIEW M1).
3. **Analytic signal.** `envelope.py:138-140` zero-pads to `next_fast_len(T)`, the smallest 5-smooth length ≥ T (`mne/filter.py:42-64, 238-247`). It then calls `scipy.signal.hilbert` (`_signaltools.py:2410, 2519-2529`: DC and Nyquist bins kept once, positive bins doubled, negative bins zeroed) and truncates back to T. Powers of two are not padded. In the probe, T=1031 is padded to 1080. Compared with an unpadded analytic signal, this changes `pec` by up to 7.1e-3 (max over the 10 pairs of z-scored iid_gaussian_T4096[:, :1031]) and by up to 2.2e-3 (max over the 3 pairs of z-scored `np.random.default_rng(3).standard_normal((3, 1031))`). An explicitly 1080-padded `scipy.signal.hilbert` reference agrees with pyspi to 5.5e-16.
4. **Envelopes.** `envelope.py:146-158`: e = |z|. With log=True, e = log(|z|²). Envelopes are centred, their norms are taken, and an exact-zero norm is set to 1.
5. **Non-orth branch.** `envelope.py:164-166, 181-183`: corr[i,k] = Σ_t e_i (e_k − ē_k) / ‖e_k − ē_k‖ / ‖e_i − ē_i‖. The seed e_i is **not centred**.
6. **Orth branch.** `envelope.py:167-178, 181-183`: o_ik = |Im(z_i · conj(z_k)/|z_k|)| = |z_i||sin(φ_i − φ_k)|, logged and squared if log=True, then centred. c_ik = Pearson(o_ik, e_k). The self row is forced constant, which gives 0 on the diagonal.
   - `:184-188` then optionally takes |C| and returns (C + Cᵀ)/2. The `absolute` flag is ignored when orth=False; the probe shows `PEC(absolute=True)` returns a matrix bit-identical to `pec`.
7. **Output.** `envelope.py:201-225` stores only `triu_indices(k=0)` with `indices="symmetric"`. `mne_connectivity/base.py:802-810` writes the stored values into both triangles, so the output is **exactly symmetric**.
   - Historical MNE 0.23 (`/home/dan/anaconda3/envs/pyspi/.../mne/connectivity/envelope.py:15-152`, read for comparison) ran the same algorithm but returned the full row-wise matrix. That is the source of the last-bit asymmetry described in REVIEW M2.

**Independent reproduction.** A from-scratch numpy reference (np.fft analytic signal plus centred Pearson, using no mne or scipy.signal) reproduced all six identifiers to ≤ 2.3e-15 on iid_gaussian, bivariate_gaussian and delayed_noise at T = 64 and 1024. The worst cases were 2.1e-14 on narrowband_f0-0.25 and 7.7e-15 on shared_envelope. The only exception is the orth variants on the duplicated-channel pair, covered below.

**Pairwise only.** Each pair's value depends only on that pair: on iid_gaussian_T1024, pair (1,3) computed from a 2-channel subset is bit-identical to the full computation.

## Deviation analysis: issues common to all units

- **No band-pass filtering.** No band-pass or carrier frequency is applied anywhere. The canonical AEC/PEC is a narrow-band, frequency-resolved quantity. Here the "envelope" is that of the broadband z-scored series, and the SPI returns one broadband value per pair. This is a definition-scope deviation, and the documentation does not mention it.
- **Sensitivity to offsets and trends.** The Hilbert envelope includes the DC bin, so it is not offset-invariant.
  - Kernel path (normalise=False), bivariate_gaussian_T1024 with offsets [0,1,−2,5]: the output changes by up to 1.32 (`pec`), 1.14 (`pec_log`) and 0.176 (`pec_orth`). Scaling alone changes it by ≤ 1.6e-15.
  - The default z-scoring removes offsets, but not trends. On trending_T1024, `pec` for pair (2,3) is 0.964 without detrending, 0.612 with detrending and 0.611 on the clean data.
- **Symmetry (elaboration of REVIEW M2, not a new finding; recorded under already_documented).** In the current environment the output is exactly symmetric for all six identifiers. The probe confirmed that the pre-mirror matrix has |C − Cᵀ| up to 5.2e-16 and that get_data discards the lower triangle. The M2 asymmetry was measured on data saved with MNE 0.23 and does not reproduce now. As a result, current lower-triangle values differ from historical ones at roundoff level.
- **Metadata.**
  - `linear` and `time-dependent` are never attached to instances.
  - `linear` is questionable for an envelope statistic, which is a nonlinear transform of the signal.
  - `time-dependent` does not describe this statistic, which compares the two signals at zero lag only.
  - `bivariate` is accurate.
  - The `unsigned` label conflicts with the signed variants: REVIEW M8 for pec/pec_log, and the same problem extends to pec_orth/pec_orth_log.

## misc.pec.orth-False_log-False (`pec`)

- **Implemented.** Broadband amplitude-envelope Pearson correlation (|z|, not |z|²).
- **Against the documentation.** The value matches "correlation between the two amplitude envelopes", even though the SPI is called "power envelope correlation".
- **Against the canonical definition.** It is broadband instead of band-limited, and it correlates amplitude rather than power. For a white Gaussian pair with ρ = 0.8, the analytic amplitude-envelope correlation is (₂F₁(−½,−½;1;ρ²) − 1)/(4/π − 1) = 0.614. The power-envelope correlation is ρ² = 0.640. A numpy Monte Carlo (200 runs × T=4096) gives 0.6149 ± 0.0142 and confirms the formula. pyspi gives 0.611 for bivariate_gaussian_T1024 pair (2,3).
- **Range.** Signed in [−1,1] in exact arithmetic (M8; probe minimum −0.441).
- **Candidate numerical defect: uncentred seed.** Because the seed is not centred (`envelope.py:165,181`), roundoff of size mean(e_i)·Σ(e_k − ē_k) is divided by two tiny norms when envelopes barely vary.
  - Probe setup: T=256, pipeline path (default z-scoring, no detrend), channels (1 + m·a(t))·sin(2π·0.25t) and (1 + m·b(t))·sin(2π·0.25t + 0.7), pair (0,1). a and b are the first 256 samples of a 45-point moving average ('valid' mode) of 300 standard normals each, drawn in that order from one `np.random.default_rng(5)` stream.

  | m | Envelope CV (channel a / b) | `pec` value (pipeline) | `pec` value (kernel, normalise=False) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 1e-6 | 1.5e-7 / 1.8e-7 | 0.06184 (centred reference: 0.06310) | 0.0576 |
  | 1e-9 | 1.5e-10 / 1.8e-10 | −1071 | +1432 |
  | 1e-12 | 1.5e-13 / 1.8e-13 | −5.3e9 | −1.65e9 |
  | 0 | 1.9e-16 / 5.2e-15 (pure sinusoids) | 1.31e14 | 0.160 |

  - The magnitudes are roundoff, so they change with the phases and the draw. The stable feature is that values leave [−1,1] with no error. On the same inputs the orthogonalised variants stay in [−1,1] (pure sinusoids: pec_orth −0.282, pec_orth_log −0.281).

  For realistic envelopes the effect is at roundoff level. The same mechanism (different numerators, not only different division order) contributes to the historical M2 asymmetry.
- **Proposed checks.**
  - Kernel tolerance 1e-12 against the from-scratch reference.
  - Analytic statistical check on bivariate_gaussian at T=4096: 0.0828 for ρ=0.3 and 0.6141 for ρ=0.8, within 3 Monte Carlo SDs (≈0.017).
  - Direction/symmetry check on shared_envelope, delayed_noise and var2_bivariate.
  - Qualitative check with constant-envelope sinusoids on the pipeline path (out of range).
  - Invariance check on scaled_offset in the pipeline, and offset dependence on the kernel path.
  - Trending on the pipeline-detrend path.
  - Padding check at T=1031.

## misc.pec.orth-True_log-False (`pec_orth`, `pec_orth_abs`)

- **Implemented.** Hipp 2012 pairwise orthogonalisation applied to amplitude envelopes of broadband analytic signals. pec_orth = (c_ik + c_ki)/2 and pec_orth_abs = (|c_ik| + |c_ki|)/2.
- **Against the documentation.**
  - "Absolute of the correlation coefficient" is applied to each direction before averaging, so pec_orth_abs ≥ |pec_orth|, and the two differ whenever the two directions have opposite signs.
  - pec_orth is signed (probe minimum −0.385). This extends M8, which names only pec and pec_log.
- **Against the canonical definition.** Broadband instead of per-carrier; amplitude instead of log power; the abs option is not in Hipp 2012.
- **Candidate defect: duplicated or proportional channels.** For bit-identical or exactly proportional channels, the orthogonalised signal is 0 in exact arithmetic. However, `Im(z_i·(conj(z_k)/|z_k|))` leaves a residue of about 1e-16 that scales with |z|, so it correlates with the envelope. The zero-variance guard only catches an exact 0. The probe on shared_envelope, where x2 is bit-identical to x0, gives these pec_orth values for pair (0,2):

  | T | pec_orth (0,2) |
  | --- | --- |
  | 64 | 0.218 |
  | 256 | 0.360 |
  | 1024 | 0.326 |
  | 4096 | 0.314 |

  The synthetic pair [a, 2a] gives 0.387. Here a and b are successive `np.random.default_rng(0).standard_normal(1024)` draws, the channels are [a, 2a, a + 0.3b, b] on the pipeline path, and the pair is (0,1). The value is roundoff and depends on the draw: two channels [a, 2a] with a = `default_rng(0).standard_normal(256)` give 0.4645. The expected value is 0, or undefined. This matters for duplicated or bridged EEG channels.
- **Null behaviour.** The analytic population value is 0 for any zero-lag-mixed Gaussian pair. pec_orth_abs is positively biased (probe iid T=1024: 0.0045–0.040).
- **Proposed checks.**
  - Kernel tolerance against the reference, excluding pair (0,2).
  - Analytic null check (|value| ≤ 3/√T on bivariate_gaussian while pec is about 0.61).
  - Qualitative duplicate-channel check.
  - Exact decomposition into directional c_ik and c_ki to settle the abs semantics.
  - Symmetry, offset, detrend and padding checks as for pec.

## misc.pec.orth-False_log-True (`pec_log`)

- **Implemented.** The Pearson correlation of log(|z|²), which equals the log-amplitude correlation. This matches the documented "squared and logs were taken".
- **Canonical comparison.** For a white Gaussian pair, the analytic value is 6·Li₂(ρ²)/π², which is 0.0560 for ρ=0.3 and 0.4803 for ρ=0.8. The Monte Carlo gives 0.0566 ± 0.0158 and 0.4808 ± 0.0158. pyspi gives 0.516 at T=1024 and 0.486 at T=64 for pair (2,3).
- **Range.** Signed (M8; probe minimum −0.378).
- **Candidate numerical defect.** The same uncentred-seed instability as pec, with the same probe signals on the pipeline path: CV 1.5e-10 gives +296.6, CV 1.5e-13 gives 1.25e8, and pure sinusoids give −1.41e13.
  - Caveat: this needs a non-zero mean log-power in the seed. On the kernel path (normalise=False) the unit-amplitude sinusoids have log(|z|²) ≈ 0, so the uncentred seed is almost centred and `pec_log` stays bounded (0.0631 at CV 1.5e-10, 0 for pure sinusoids), while `pec` remains unstable.
  - Z-scoring raises the amplitude to about √2 (mean log-power 0.689), which exposes the instability. On the default pipeline any near-constant-amplitude input can reach it.
- **Untested hypothesis.** An exact zero of z would give log(0) = −inf and a NaN row. In practice this cannot happen after z-scoring.
- **Proposed checks.** As for pec, with the log-power analytic targets.

## misc.pec.orth-True_log-True (`pec_orth_log`, `pec_orth_log_abs`)

- **Closest to Hipp 2012.** It uses the same orthogonalisation, log power and two-direction average. It differs only in being broadband, in the Hilbert padding, and in having the abs option.
- **Range.** pec_orth_log is signed (probe minimum −0.337). pec_orth_log_abs lies in [0,1] and is positively biased under the null (0.0060–0.0465 on iid T=1024).
- **Duplicated channels.** The duplicate-channel defect also appears here: shared_envelope pair (0,2) gives 0.181, 0.339, 0.294 and 0.241 at the four T values, and [a, 2a] gives 0.443 with the same draw as above (0.3923 for the 256-sample two-channel draw). The log of the squared residue (about 1e-32) stays finite because the probe found no exact zeros (0 of 1024 samples).
- **Proposed checks.** Same set as the orth unit.

## Open questions

1. **Hipp 2012 details.** I have not re-read the paper to confirm that it correlates log-transformed power envelopes and averages both orthogonalisation directions; these statements come from memory and agree with MNE's code. It is also open whether Khan et al. 2018 used `absolute=True` and log power.
2. **The REVIEW M2 table.** It reports roundoff asymmetry for `pec` and `pec_log` from historical saved data. Should storage and regression notes record that the current environment gives exactly symmetric output, so the lower triangle no longer matches historical bits at about 1e-16?
3. **Should pyspi band-pass before the Hilbert transform?** Should it expose a band parameter as the spectral SPIs do, or document the broadband definition explicitly? Interpreting results on EEG depends on this.
4. **Duplicated channels.** What policy should apply to bit-identical or proportional channels in the orth variants (NaN, 0, or a warning)? Are such channels present in the EEG data, for example bridged electrodes or reference duplicates?
5. **Constant envelopes.** The uncentred-seed defect needs envelopes that are almost constant (CV ≲ 1e-6; for `pec_log` also a non-zero mean log-power, which z-scoring supplies), so it is unlikely in EEG. It still produces values outside [−1,1] with no warning. It is open whether to fix this upstream or guard in pyspi, for example by centring or clipping.
6. **"abs" semantics.** Should "abs" mean the mean of absolute directional coefficients, which is the current behaviour, or |pec_orth|? The documentation is ambiguous.

**Reference.** The reference is `_kernels_basic_misc.envelope_correlation`. It implements Hipp et al. 2012 from numpy and scipy primitives: the analytic signal from `scipy.signal.hilbert`, zero-padded to the next 5-smooth length as mne does, and a centred Pearson correlation. It never imports mne or mne_connectivity, so it is independent of the delegate. It does share one primitive with the delegate: `scipy.signal.hilbert`, which mne also calls. The independence rule allows this as a numpy/scipy building block. Still, it is weaker than the reference proposed at the trace stage, which was np.fft only with no scipy.signal. To close that gap, the scratchpad probe `pec_check/npfft_only_ref.py` builds the analytic signal from `np.fft` alone (5-smooth padding, h multiplier, ifft, truncation) and applies it to the input pyspi actually saw. It agrees with pyspi on all 114 kern/pipe/invar/detrend/trendraw/offsetkern runs of the six identifiers to ≤ 9.3e-14 (pec 3.1e-14, pec_log 3.9e-15, pec_orth 5.3e-16, pec_orth_log 9.3e-14; for the orth variants the bit-identical shared_envelope pair is excluded). On all 24 stat/lagstat runs it agrees to ≤ 1.2e-14.

- **Orth evaluation order.** By default the orth reference uses the definitional order in explicit real arithmetic, (Im a_i·Re a_j − Re a_i·Im a_j)/|a_j| (`mne_order=False`; `_kernels_basic_misc.py:583-586`). This is exactly 0 for a bit-identical copy. The same quantity formed through a complex product, Im(a_i·conj a_j)/|a_j|, is **not** exactly 0 on this machine. With numpy 2.5.4 the complex multiply leaves a residue of up to 1.05e-16 at all 1024 of 1024 samples of shared_envelope T1024 (scratchpad `pec_check/complex_product_residue.py`, adapted from the review probe). `mne_order=True` evaluates a_i·(conj(a_j)/|a_j|), as mne does (`envelope.py:147-148, 168`). It is used only to show where pyspi's value for duplicated channels comes from. `refs/pec.py:orth_complex_product` gives the complex-product form; its values appear only as extra keys (`ref_ref_complex_product_order_02` / `_01`).
- **`refs/pec.py:directional`.** This gives the orthogonalised coefficients c_ij before symmetrisation.
- **Analytic values for white Gaussian pairs with zero-lag correlation ρ.** The analytic signals are circular complex Gaussian with coherence ρ.
  - Amplitude: (₂F₁(−½,−½;1;ρ²) − 1)/(4/π − 1).
  - Log power: 6·Li₂(ρ²)/π².
  - Orthogonalised signed variants: 0.
- **delayed_noise.** The analytic signal of white noise has lag-d coherence 2/(πd) for odd d and 0 for even d (the discrete Hilbert kernel). With the 0.1 noise, the (0,k) coherence is 2/(π d_k)/√1.01.
- **Standard errors.** These come from a 400-replicate numpy Monte Carlo of the kernel on fresh draws, z-scored like the pipeline. A statistical run passes if |pyspi − value| ≤ 2·sd at every pair. This is compare.py's documented rule, with se = the Monte Carlo SD and no multiplicity correction, so compare's `max_z` is the raw z. An earlier version of this check stored se = sd·z*/2, with z* = Φ⁻¹(1 − 0.025/m) a Bonferroni factor over the m unique pairs (2.638 for 6 pairs, 2.807 for 10). That widened the pass band and let four runs pass that exceed 2 SD. The adversarial review flagged it, and the reference now uses the plain rule. z* is still reported, as `ref_z_star_bonferroni` and `ref_bonferroni_pass`, for information only. With 6–10 pairs per run, a single-draw exceedance at one pair is expected in roughly a quarter to a third of the 24 runs if the pairs were independent. Five fail, four of them on the same pair of the same draw.
- **Analytic formulas against the Monte Carlo.** The formulas agree with the Monte Carlo means:

| Run | Analytic | MC mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bivariate_gaussian T4096, pair (2,3), pec | 0.6141 | 0.6142 |
| bivariate_gaussian T4096, pair (2,3), pec_log | 0.4803 | 0.4804 |
| delayed_noise T4096, pair (0,1), pec | 0.3775 | 0.3774 |
| delayed_noise T4096, pair (0,1), pec_log | 0.2741 | 0.2738 |

The table omits the ρ = 0.3 pair, bivariate_gaussian T4096 (0,1). There the 400-replicate kernel Monte Carlo mean is 0.0847 (sd 0.0190, standard error of the mean 0.00095), 2.0 standard errors above the analytic amplitude value 0.0828. For pec_log it is 0.0571 against 0.0560 (1.3 standard errors). A direct 4e7-sample draw of circular complex Gaussian pairs with coherence 0.3, with no Hilbert step, gives 0.08276 (standard error 0.00013 over 40 blocks) against the analytic 0.08282, and 0.05593 (0.00014) against 0.05600 (scratchpad `pec_check/rho03_direct_mc.py`). So the formulas hold and the 2-standard-error gap is Monte Carlo noise.

### Common to all four units

**Symmetry.** max|A − Aᵀ| is 0 exactly on every run (`ref_pyspi_max_asym`).

- This elaborates REVIEW M2, which is already documented: mne-connectivity 0.9.0 mirrors the stored upper triangle, so the historical asymmetry does not reproduce.
- The trace probe's figure for the pre-mirror asymmetry (5.2e-16) was not re-measured here.

**Preprocessing.**

- *Pipeline invariance.* pyspi on `scaled_offset` matches the reference on the unscaled `bivariate_gaussian` draw (`*-invar`, max_abs ≤ 1.3e-15).
- *Kernel path, no z-scoring.* pyspi matches the raw-input reference (`*-offsetkern`, ≤ 6.3e-17 for the orth variants, ≤ 6.7e-16 overall).
- *Offset effect.* On the reference side, adding offsets [0,1,−2,5] to bivariate_gaussian_T1024 changes the matrix by 1.32 (pec), 1.14 (pec_log), 0.176 (pec_orth), 0.175 (pec_orth_abs) and 0.107 (both orth_log variants). Scaling alone changes it by ≤ 2e-15. These figures reproduce the trace-stage numbers.
- *Trending data, pec pair (2,3).* The plain pipeline gives 0.964 (`*-trendraw`), the detrended pipeline 0.612 (`*-detrend`), and the clean data 0.611. The detrend path matches the lstsq-detrended reference to ≤ 2.8e-14.

**Hilbert padding.** At T = 1031, MNE pads the Hilbert FFT to 1080 points. The padded reference reproduces pyspi to ≤ 5.4e-16 (`*-pad1031`). The unpadded analytic signal differs by:

| Identifier | Unpadded − padded |
| --- | --- |
| pec | 0.00714 |
| pec_log | 0.00933 |
| pec_orth | 0.00373 |
| pec_orth_log | 0.0157 |

All audit T values are powers of two, so no padding happens there.

**No band-pass anywhere.** Every kernel-path and pipeline-path comparison matches a reference that applies no filter. The definition-scope candidate is therefore confirmed: these are broadband envelopes.

**Metadata.** The scratchpad probe `pec_check/labels_probe.py` shows that all six instances carry `['unsigned','misc','undirected']`. ## Open questions: answered

1. **Hipp 2012 details.** Answered from the PMC author manuscript (PMC3861400, Online Methods).
   - Y⊥X(t,f) = imag(Y(t,f)·X(t,f)*/|X(t,f)|).
   - Power is the squared magnitude of the complex Morlet-wavelet estimate (log-spaced carrier frequencies, 2–128 Hz in quarter-octave steps), log-transformed "to render the power statistics more normal".
   - The Pearson correlation is computed for both orthogonalisation directions and the two values are averaged.
   - pec_orth_log therefore matches Hipp except for the broadband analytic signal.
   - Whether Khan et al. 2018 used `absolute=True` and log power was not checked. Reading Khan 2018's Methods would settle it.
2. **REVIEW M2 table.** Yes, it should be noted. Every pec run here gives max|A − Aᵀ| = 0 exactly, because mne-connectivity 0.9.0 keeps only the upper triangle and mirrors it. Lower-triangle values therefore differ from historical MNE 0.23 output at roundoff level. Storage or regression comparisons against historical files should compare the upper triangle only, or allow about 1e-14.
3. **Band-pass.** This is a policy question that the checks cannot decide. They establish what pyspi computes: a broadband envelope correlation. On narrowband or carrier-dominated data the broadband envelope approximates the band envelope. On broadband EEG it mixes bands, and on white data it also picks up Hilbert-kernel coupling between lag-shifted copies: pec on delayed_noise (0,1) is 0.385 at a 1-sample lag, matching the analytic 0.3775.
   - Either option needs a code change, not a reinterpretation: exposing a band parameter, or band-passing before the SPI as the EEG workflow does for the spectral SPIs.
   - At minimum the documentation should state "broadband".
4. **Duplicated or proportional channels.** These are confirmed to give 0.1–0.44 in the orth variants (tables above) instead of 0.
   - The checks cannot say whether such channels occur in the EEG data. Settling that needs a scan for bridged or duplicate electrodes, for example an exact-equality or a correlation > 0.999 check per recording.
   - The value comes from the delegate forming the orthogonal part in complex floating-point arithmetic. It is not specific to mne's divide-first order: a complex-product form Im(z_i·conj z_j)/|z_j| leaves the same kind of residue on this machine (0.068–0.365 for pec_orth, 0.012–0.307 for pec_orth_log on shared_envelope (0,2)).
   - Upstream, mne-connectivity would get exactly 0 only by writing the explicit real form (Im z_i·Re z_j − Re z_i·Im z_j)/|z_j|. Alternatively it could treat |orthogonal part| ≤ c·eps·|z_i| as zero. pyspi could instead set pairs with a zero-lag coherence of 1 to NaN, or warn.
5. **Uncentred seed.** This needs envelope CV ≲ 1e-6 to matter: the deviation is 1.25e-3 at CV 1.5e-7 and grows to out-of-range values at CV 1.5e-10. Realistic data stays at roundoff (≤ 3.1e-14). The cleanest fix is upstream (centre the seed at `envelope.py:165`). A pyspi-side guard could clip to [−1,1] or set |r| > 1 to NaN, but clipping would hide the problem. The decision belongs to the maintainers.
6. **"abs" semantics.** The implemented meaning is settled: the mean of the absolute directional coefficients, confirmed to ≤ 2.2e-16. It is not |pec_orth|, and it is positively biased under independence (about 0.013 at T = 4096). Which meaning the identifier should carry is a documentation decision; the supplement should state the per-direction form.
7. **Exact zero in the analytic signal.** Confirmed for an all-zero channel on the kernel path: pec_log and all orth variants give a NaN row and column, while pec gives 0. A zero at a single sample was not constructed; it is practically unreachable after z-scoring, and the pipeline rejects constant channels.
